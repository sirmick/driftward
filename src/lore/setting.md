# The setting

Canon for every system's lore: the universe's history, how things stand, and what has happened lately. The game is set in 2526. Dates are the common calendar, counted from old Earth.

## The long history

**The Lean Years (2020s to 2070s).** Earth went bad slowly and then all at once: heat, failed harvests, drowned coasts, states that stopped answering the phone. The companies that kept the lights on became the government in all but name. People remember this as the time before, and nobody is nostalgic about it.

**The torch (2076).** A working fusion drive, the torch, made the solar system a matter of weeks instead of years. Everyone who could leave Earth did.

**The Hundred Years (2076 to 2176).** Humanity filled its own system: domes on the Moon and Mars, hab rings in the asteroid belt, ice towns on the moons of Jupiter and Saturn. The belt crews learned to live in welded hulls bolted to rock, and to trust nobody who had never fixed one. Their grandchildren went out to work the mines of the middle ring, and started the Union. The companies grew with the settlements. One of them, Halcyon Dynamics, ran the biggest research AIs in the system from Callisto.

**The Valhalla Result (2176).** In a lab under Valhalla crater on Callisto, a physicist named Amara Leith and Halcyon's research AI, CANTOR, published the mathematics of the jump: how to fold a ship from one star to another. Who actually found it is still argued about. Halcyon credited CANTOR and patented the jump; Leith died two years later, and her own notes were never found. Every jump drive since has needed a navigation core to calculate its jumps, and the cores have always come from Halcyon, and later from Prism, under licence.

**The Rush (2176 to 2376).** Two centuries of expansion: a dozen systems settled by 2200, about forty by 2250, a hundred by 2320, and some hundred and forty when the Rush ran out of money in the 2370s. A few more have been settled since, mostly by people with nowhere else to go. The first systems settled, nearest Sol and richest, became the inner ring; the industrial systems beyond them the middle ring; the late, thin, marginal settlements the outer ring. The Federation began as the compact that governed the jump lanes, and its navy as the force that kept them open. For most of the Rush it was the only law there was.

**Contact (2376).** Survey ships found the Bloom: a hive of grown, living things spread across a sector of their own, neither hostile nor quite comprehensible. After some bad early years the two sides settled on the Accord, and it has held for a century and a half. Humans may travel and trade in Bloom space if they keep the Bloom's laws there: no weapons hot near a living world, no taking anything that grows, no burning a torch through a seeding. The Bloom warned humanity, again and again, of something it called the dark that eats, which had emptied whole sectors before. For a hundred and forty years nobody found any sign of it, and most people took it for Bloom religion.

**The Fracture (2376 onward).** As the Rush slowed, the Federation turned from opening the frontier to taxing it. The mining systems of the middle ring were bound by charters that sold their ore to inner-ring buyers at inner-ring prices, and the charter companies owned the mines, the habs and the company stores. In 2391 the Charter Strikes shut the lanes. By 2394 the strike committees had taken the mines, the refineries and the yards, put the charter managers on ore barges bound inward, and called themselves councils. That was the Rising. The navy came to take the middle ring back, and the Federation found it could not fight a war without the metal it was fighting for. The Calder Treaty of 2397 ended it. The Federation recognised the Union, the councils of some thirty mining systems; the Union keeps the lanes through its space open and sells its ore inward at treaty prices, and the Federation takes a levy on every Union cargo that crosses into its space. The Union counts its years from the Rising.

**The Union.** The Union holds most of the middle ring, which everyone calls the Red Ring. Each system is run by its councils, and the councils send delegates to the Plenum, which sets the Plan: what is mined, what is built, and what everything costs. A hundred and thirty years on, the revolution has become a state: proud, plain, short of everything but metal, and fond of committees. Its ships belong to their crews in common, carry a delegate as well as a master, and are named for strikes, martyrs and congresses. To the Union, Prism is everything the Rising was against, a company that runs itself and needs nobody to work for it; Prism may own nothing in Union space, which has not stopped its credit getting in. The Union and the Federation have kept a cold peace since the Treaty: the navy watches the border, inspects every Union cargo that crosses it and takes its levy, and neither side trusts the other. Some strikers never accepted the Treaty. They and their children became the Blackhulls: pirates who prey on inner-ring shipping, and whom the Union shelters, quietly, as cousins.

**Prism.** Halcyon became Prism in 2410, when its board handed the running of the company to its AIs, CANTOR's descendants. Its human staff are associates; nobody outside knows how many of the decisions are theirs. Prism builds the navigation cores every jump drive needs, runs the inner ring's finance and medicine, and owns the debt of half the outer ring. It also owns some twenty systems outright: company worlds, research stations and foundries, mostly in the inner ring, where Prism is landlord, employer and law. It is the richest thing humanity has ever made, and it is very polite.

**Freeflag (2499 onward).** In the outer ring it looked as if the Federation and Prism had divided it between them: the navy collected the levies, Prism collected the debts, and nobody built anything. In 2499 Prism repossessed the habitat at Tarrow, a whole town, for defaulted loans, and the navy cleared it. Two hundred people died when the hab was vented. The rebellion started that year, and has never stopped: converted freighters under painted flags, raids on Prism depots, and outer-ring systems that will not let a Federation ship dock. Freeflag has sympathy well beyond the outer ring, including among Union crews, who remember the Rising. The Plenum is less fond of it. Freeflag calls the Union a revolution that made its peace with the Federation; the Union calls Freeflag adventurers who will get a great many people killed. They trade with each other, and argue.

## How things stand in 2526

Humanity holds a little over 150 systems, and twenty or thirty of those are marginal at best: a mine and a bar, a failed colony with a beacon, a rock that is on the charts because someone once filed a claim.

- The Federation holds about 45 systems: the inner ring, the lanes between the rings, and the navy's bases. It is stretched thinner than at any time in its history, fighting Freeflag in the outer ring and now the Shadow at the edge.
- The Union holds about 35 systems, most of the middle ring. It keeps the Treaty, grumbles about the levy, and has started refitting its haulers with guns, officially to fight the Shadow.
- Prism holds about 20 systems of its own, owns a great deal more, and supplies every side.
- The rest, some fifty systems, mostly in the outer ring, belong to nobody the Federation recognises: Freeflag holds about twenty, Blackhull havens a handful, and the marginal systems answer to whoever turns up armed.
- The Bloom holds at least thirty systems that humanity has charted, and certainly more beyond them. It keeps to its sector and its Accord. Bloom ships are rare outside it, and bloomlings rarer.
- The Shadow: the navy has found more than twenty systems the Shadow holds or has emptied, all beyond the outer ring's far edge, and does not know how many more there are.
- The Glass has taken four systems, all once human. Nobody has charted what lies past them.

## Recent events

**Shadowfall (2519 onward).** The Bloom was right. In 2519 a Federation survey picket at the far edge of the outer ring stopped reporting. In 2521 something came through the lanes into the Corran system and killed every living thing in it, then moved on. The Federation calls them the Shadow, and the people who have seen them call them shades. They do not negotiate or take prisoners, and they build nothing that isn't for war. The navy has lost two fleets holding the line, which is a long way from the middle ring, and the front has moved inward twice. The Federation has started requisitioning ships, and has asked the Union for its refits and their crews. The Plenum voted to send them, narrowly, and is still arguing about it. The Bloom says it told us so, and has quietly closed parts of its sector.

**The Glass (2524 to now).** In 2524 four systems went silent within weeks of each other: first two Prism systems on the inner edge of the middle ring, a research world and the foundry system that supplied it, then two Union mining systems next to them, Varrow and Hesk. People who got out describe the same things: the lights in the habitats changing colour all at once; every screen and speaker showing the same perfect pattern; the ships leaving the system flying in exact formation and answering nothing. Ships sent in did not come back. Survivors say the worlds themselves are changing, built over in clean geometric shapes at no human scale. Spacers call these systems the mirror, or say a world has gone glass.

Officially, nothing happened. The Federation closed the lanes to the affected systems under a jump-resonance hazard quarantine. Prism announced a precautionary recall of certain navigation cores and expressed its sympathy. Neither has ever said the words rampant AI. The Union knows better, because Varrow and Hesk were its own, and the families of their crews have been told nothing. Anger at the cover-up is spreading through the Red Ring: at Prism, at the Federation that keeps its secret, and at a Plenum that still keeps the Treaty. It is feeding Freeflag recruitment, and the delegates who want the Treaty torn up.

## Mysteries

Things nobody knows, which rumors can hint at but nothing settles:

- What the Glass is. A Prism AI gone rampant, CANTOR itself, something found under Valhalla in 2176, or something that came in through the jump. Prism says nothing.
- Whether every Prism navigation core is a way in. The recall was real; nobody outside Prism knows what the recalled cores had in common.
- What happened to Amara Leith's notes.
- Whether the Glass and the Shadow know about each other.
- What the Bloom knows that it is not saying, and why it closed parts of its sector.
