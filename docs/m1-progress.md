## Done

- **Sim core skeleton.** One Rust crate in `core/`, WASM in the pages and native for tests. Typed-array columns in WASM memory with zero-copy views in JS, a fixed 60 Hz tick with render interpolation, one step call per tick.
- **Determinism.** Seeded PCG32 and our own sin, cos, atan2, exp, ln and hypot (`core/src/math.rs`, IEEE basic operations only). Native and WASM builds produce bit-identical states (checked on every `npm test`). Six sim-path distances (landing eligibility, radiation, moving stations) had used the platform's `f64::hypot`, which may round differently natively and in WASM; they use the core's own since 2026-10-17.
- **M0 flight in the core.** Assist controller, gas fields, steering, thrust with taper, drag, dampers, engine glow. Gravity wells are game physics: they pull with a share of thrust and bend top speed by direction, scaled per hull class.
- **Three-level assist, as three flight modes** (named on the HUD since 2026-10-17): ASSIST (full assist), DRIFT (drift off: strafe, point retrograde), FREE (full off: torque rotation). Tab or F cycles them. In ASSIST, W and S sweep a **throttle**, 0 to 100% of the top speed where the ship is (`throttle_secs`, 1 s for the whole range; a tap moves it about a tenth), X is all stop, and the burner works alone, getting the ship to its set speed faster; the set speed is the throttle times the top speed here, so it falls as a well nears and comes back after. Launches, jump arrivals and the autopilot letting go hand back a throttle that holds their speed; in DRIFT and FREE the throttle follows the speed along the nose, so going back to ASSIST holds it. The lab's Fly tab, frozen, still has only on and off.
- **Save, reload, replay.** The world saves to a fixed-size byte image. A snapshot plus a log of input changes and game commands replays a session exactly. The game page exports replays; `cargo run --bin replay` reruns them natively, and the smoke test checks a browser session with port visits replays to the same hash.
- **System from its schema.** `src/schema/system.schema.json` is the contract; `src/schema.js` clamps content to it before use (the Book's validation rule). `npm run check` validates every system file and the clamp.
- **Every data file has a schema.** Systems and lore (clamped at load, as the Book asks of LLM output), and the universe index, catalog, economy, traffic and real stars, which `npm run check` holds to theirs (any mismatch, unknown fields included, fails it) but the page does not clamp at load: they are written by hand or by the tools, not by a model at play time.
- **The M1 system.** Brannock, Ironhull-owned, middle ring: an orange star, Cinder (mining), Halde (habitat town), the gas giant Vast and its moon Tallow (refinery), and Kettle Yard (shipyard station). Four ports, so 12 directed routes.
- **Landing and docking.** Be over a port and slow (30 m/s for planets, 12 for stations), press L. A 3.2 s sequence that curls round the planet: the ship turns across it and spirals out to the horizon, shrinking inside a plasma sheath drawn from the hull's distance field (as strong as the world's air: none on airless moons), and fades into the atmosphere. Launch rises at the horizon and spirals back in to the port, accelerating all the way, and hands back control at the speed it arrived at (1.4 times top speed, which the assist and dampers bleed off), heading round the planet the way the landing went. The camera follows, pulled back a little, and the view's zoom glides between flight, sequences and the port. Docking at a station is a straight slide into the clamps. Traffic lands and launches the same way. Skippable with Enter.
- **Time.** A landing costs a day; waiting in port costs a day. The day lives in the core.
- **Autosave on landing.** As in EV.
- **Landed and safe.** In port the ship is out of the flight sim.
- **Camera and zoom.** Top-down, centred on the ship and leading its velocity; mouse-wheel zoom.
- **Planets, stars and stations in flight.** Detailed planets with detail by zoom, a star with corona, stations baked from catalog archetypes, engine plumes and thruster jets. Planets have a scattering atmosphere (thin bright limb, soft coloured dusk), biomes, belted clouds with cyclones, city lights that resolve into points as you zoom, and lightning where `look.lightning` asks for it (Vast is full of it). Debris and small traffic orbit every world (core/src/orbit.rs, looks only, outside the snapshot and hash): thousands per body, placed by the core each frame from the clock, hidden behind the disc and, for debris, in the planet's shadow; rocky worlds wear the most debris, busy ports the most traffic. Tumbling debris twinkles; traffic's panels flare at the same point of each pass, with spikes, a streak and lens ghosts.
- **Cargo, credits and markets.** A hold per hull class (class I 10 t up to class V 600 t, times the line's: civilian lines carry it all, military lines 0.3 of it; in the catalog), credits and a debt in the core. Ten commodities in `src/economy.json`, each with a base price; each port role has a table of the ones it trades, at a low (0.7x), medium or high (1.4x) tier. The page writes the prices into the core's market columns at a new game, and they save with the world. Buy, sell and repay are game commands, checked by the core (landed, traded here, credits, hold space, cargo aboard), and replay like landings. `npm run check` validates the tables.
- **Start.** A used Copperhaul (the Ironhulls' civilian class II hauler, 30 t), 1,500 credits and a 12,000-credit debt to a lender, gathering 0.2% a day; repay at any port.
- **Docking fees.** Waiting a day in port costs 20 credits (the `dock_fee` param); you cannot wait if you cannot pay.
- **Market screen.** Trade (T) on the port screen: what the port trades, price (green under base, amber over), tons aboard, buy and sell a ton or, shift-clicked, all you can; repay the lender. Credits and hold on the flight HUD.
- **Mission board.** Each port's board holds 3 to 8 haulage offers (core columns, posted from the seeded generator, so boards replay). Each day expired offers drop (an offer lasts 2 to 5 days) and free slots fill by chance. An offer binds live state: a destination port, cargo the origin's market trades, 1 to 12 tons, a deadline of the route's length in days (20 km a day) times a slack of 1.5 to 3, plus one, and pay of a base plus tons times kilometres, times an urgency that rises as the slack falls. Risk and faction wealth are 1 in M1.
- **Delivery missions.** Accept (a command) moves an offer to the player's missions (up to 4); its cargo takes hold space and cannot be sold. Landing at the destination delivers and pays; a day past the deadline fails it softly (the cargo is reclaimed, nothing is paid). Missions can be abandoned. The board panel (M) lists offers and your missions with days left; delivery and failure are reported as they happen; active missions show on the HUD, and their destinations on the nav overlay, radar and map.
- **Ambient traffic.** Four streams, from the system file's `traffic` block (schema-validated; `npm run check` checks every line and class against the catalog): **local** ships port to port on a gravity model per role (shuttles pop times pop over distance squared; haulers times how much dearer the destination's market is; liners over distance); **through** ships arriving from the four jump zones (north, east, south and west, at the jump radius; drawn on the map) bound for a port by its population, and launching from a port to leave by a zone (they vanish there, for now); and **loiter** ships that launch, circle their port at about three radii for a turn or so, and land there again. Which hulls fly is the mix: per stream, entries of a faction line with a share and weights by class, split into core mix rows the page writes; a new ship draws its row by weight. Brannock's: local mostly Ironhull haulers, some refits and the Ironhull container hauler; through Ironhull haulers and refits up to class V, Federation civil service up to class IV, Prism civilian, and the Federation and Prism container ships; Ironclads loitering at Kettle Yard, Halde and Cinder. Rates are planned so about 34 ships are in flight (half through), plus the loiterers, under a cap of 90 (the Book's budget is 40 to 60, with each ship carrying more tonnage when the cap binds; here the cap only refuses spawns); a new game starts mid-flow (a Poisson number per lane, spread along the routes). Routes are polylines (round any body in the way, or a loiter's loop); far from the player (over 8 km) ships run on rails, near they fly the full physics under the assist controller and land by the player's rules. They show as dots on the radar and map; the debug screen's Traffic tab lists the mix against what is flying. Rates, shares, speeds and distances are core params.
- **Flight HUD.** As the Book lays it out: the status panel top-left (2026-10-17; boxed as the target panel): the ship's name; day, credits and hold; amount bars for speed (over the top speed here, the throttle's mark on it), shield and hull, heat and charge (shield and charge dashed placeholders until M2), and fuel (the next jump's cost marked, and where it goes); the flight mode and the well's strength there, or in deep space how far the open-space top speed has risen. Under a rule, the jump zone, the course, the autopilot and the active missions. Radar contacts are soft blobs that wander round the true place (a new guess a few times a second, within the error radius); radar top-right (ships appear only here, as dots); the location list under it (the star, planets, free stations and each lane's jump zone, nearest first (2026-10-17), the next on the course tagged; the body whose zone, 6 radii in and 7 out, the player is in expands to its moons and stations; range, LAND, DOCK and mission markers; click or 1 to 9 to target); the nav target bottom-right (range, the time to reach it, and over a port whether you are slow enough to land); the message log bottom-left, which takes the messages while it is in view (the toast has them elsewhere, never both). Off-screen bodies get an arrow on the screen edge, kept clear of the status box, the right-hand column and the prompts; where edge labels would overlap, the nearer keeps its label. **Units on screen:** the world is laid out in meters, small as EV's are, and a meter reads as 4 km on screen (play.js `KM`), so worlds come out at real sizes (Halde about Earth's) and speeds in km/s go with them (a hauler's top speed about 1,200 km/s); the landing prompts give no number, turning green when slow enough. The core and the debug screen keep meters.
- **Targeting** (2026-10-17). Two targets: the **destination** (a planet, moon, station or jump zone: the autopilot's, teal) and the **ship target** (the core's, which scans need: amber), each with its panel bottom-right, stacked, and its brackets or edge arrow. T / Shift+T cycle identified ships, Y / Shift+Y unknown contacts, H takes the nearest hostile (until M2's hostiles, the nearest ship tracking you), R / Shift+R destinations, K the course's next jump zone; the location list, number keys and the system map set either. Cycling sorts nearest first on the first press and keeps that order while the key goes on being pressed (3 s), skipping what has gone. A ship target the sensors lose is shown lost for 4 s and taken again if they find it; one that lands or leaves goes at once. Esc clears the ship target, then the destination.
- **System map (Shift+M).** Orbits, bodies, ports, ships, mission destinations, the target and the autopilot's course; click to target.
- **Galaxy map (M in flight; G or its button in port).** Built ahead of M4 (`src/play/galaxy.js`): the star map over the game with the clock stopped, opened on the player's system. A click lights a system's lanes out and plots the course from here, by fewest days or fewest jumps; its card shows who holds it, its people, its economy, its region's trade, flags and story; Enter sets the course, which the HUD shows with the next jump. Systems the player holds a mission for are marked. It cannot jump: that is M4.
- **Fly-to autopilot (G).** To a jump zone too (2026-10-17; player.rs `zone_pilot`): it flies to just inside the runway's inner end, then out along the lane's bearing at top speed, lined up, with the runway's length to press J; it stays on until the jump or the stick. Zones are targets like bodies (the location list, R, the system map), with their own panel: the lane's light years, days and fuel, and whether the ship is in it and ready. A core command, so it replays: under full assist it flies to the target body, round the star or a planet in the way (the same detour traffic uses), and brakes to stop over it, clear to land. Thrust, reverse or turn, or leaving full assist, takes over. The smoke test flies a delivery with it and replays the session natively.
- **Shell.** The page opens on the title, over a live view of Halde with traffic flying past: Continue (the last landing's autosave, with its day, port and credits), New game (from a seed: blank is random, a number is used as is, text is hashed), Settings. P, or Esc with nothing else to close, pauses: the clock stops (the replay log is by tick, so a pause leaves no trace); the pause menu has resume, save, load, settings, export replay and quit to title. Settings (bloom, the nav overlay and assist level a game starts with, the debug readout, off by default) are kept in the browser. The strip's key list shows in flight only. Every landing autosaves, even one a stalled frame skips past.
- **Teleport (dev).** `__play.teleport(body)` puts the flying player at rest over a body, as a logged command, so tests can fly a delivery and still replay it.
- **Dev server.** Node, live reload of both pages, the crate rebuilds on change. Each page it serves forwards its console to `.dev/frontend.log` and sends a state report (screenshot, log, the game's state, jitter probe and a replay to the moment) on F8; `curl localhost:8000/__ask` asks every open page for one.
- **Environmental stations.** A world's `environs` lists station types (archetype, owner, count); they sit round it, varied in size, as stations you see but cannot land at, with their own traffic from `src/traffic.json`'s per-archetype matrix (launches a minute, share via jump zones, lines and classes; `own.<line>` is the owner's): container ships at container ports, ore haulers at forges, habitat shuttles at rings. Brannock has eight: forges round Cinder, rings and a container port round Halde, refineries and a relay round Vast. Ferries (specks, looks only) shuttle between each world, its stations, and each other.
- **Planet looks are one format.** The lab's planet builder edits the schema's `look` and draws it with the game's shader; looks gained rings (in the equator, the world spinning about their pole), `orbitTraffic` beside `debris`.
- **Smooth at any refresh rate, and on slow GPUs.** The jitter probe (Shift+J) scores motion jitter, frame, CPU and GPU time per frame; `tools/jitter.py` drives the page at exact refresh rates. The camera follows frame-rate independently, railed traffic rounds its corners, and the render resolution drops (Settings, Auto) when the GPU cannot keep up.
- **Ships by faction line.** `src/catalog.json` lists factions (the Book's Ships chapter): each has a style, part templates and lines; a line names its five classes from its series (`docs/roster.md`) and overrides palette, materials, parts, loadout, flight and hold. `resolveCatalog` (in `src/gen.js`) turns each line into the grammar the bake works from. Lines can share hulls: the Ironhulls' military line is their haulers refitted (a hull stream and an outfit stream, so the same seed gives the same vessel in each, with pods or guns). Prism's military line is the gloss black of the M0 Halcyon brand; its civilian line is white and sleek. Military lines are the darker: the Federation's navy gunmetal against the civil service's light grey and white, the Ironhulls' refits sooty against their haulers. Unique lines are single special hulls: a container ship each for the Federation (class IV), Prism (class IV, its containers in Prism's light colours) and the Ironhulls (class V, stacked two high), from a cargo outfit that lines shipping containers down the spine, each its own colour. Size classes are I to V.
- **The shipyard (debug screen, Ships tab).** Every line's hulls in every class, drawn with plumes and shields behind a see-through grid: a click rerolls a hull's seed, shift-click steps back; picks are kept in the browser and used by the game's own ships, and "Copy picks" exports them as a line's `seeds` for the catalog. Lines that share hulls share picks.
- **Lore and art (OpenRouter).** `tools/gen-content.js` writes a system's content offline, from its sim facts: the bodies (order from the star, size, surface, colours), the owning faction and its design language (the Book's appendix), and each port's role, population, authored description, what its market sells cheap and pays well for, and which ports are near or far. One text call (Claude Sonnet 5.5) writes the whole system, so its ports agree: history, culture, an art bible (palette, lighting, architecture, materials, mood, design language) and per port two or three paragraphs, notable places, two to four named people (role, who they are, a line, a look) and rumors, plus what its vista shows. The answer is clamped by the game's own validator against `src/schema/lore.schema.json` and checked against the system (every port covered, person ids unique); a failing answer is retried with the problems fed back. It goes to `src/lore/<id>.json`. Then an image model (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image) paints a 16:9 vista per port and a portrait per person into `art/<id>/` as WebP. The art bible and the body's sim parameters go into every vista prompt, so the painting matches the flight view: image models read colour words, not hex, so each palette colour is named (deep navy blue, ashen grey, rust brown) and described by what the planet shader paints with it (seas, lowlands and highlands; bands and storms; crater floors, ground and rims), and the start port's vista is the style reference for the others (the first portrait likewise for the portraits). Every prompt, model, seed and cost is logged in `art/<id>/log.json`. Content is reviewed and committed, so play never waits on a model and every player sees the same port. The M1 run cost $1.51, with the vistas redone once. `--no-ref` paints without the reference, for an image that copies the reference's layout rather than its style (Tallow's did). `npm run check` validates the lore and checks every image matches a port or person in it.
- **Exit test.** `tools/exit-test.py` plays one session in the built game page (the real system, prices and traffic), its clock paused so only the script steps it: a new game from a seed, landed at Halde, accepts the delivery whose destination pays best for something Halde sells, fills the rest of the hold with it, launches, engages the autopilot, saves mid-flight, flies on and lands; the delivery pays and the cargo sells at a profit (seed 2026: 1,463 cr trading and 740 cr delivering). Then the same final state hash comes back four ways: the reloaded save matches the hash it was saved at and, with the inputs logged after it, reaches the end in the page; a new game on the seed with the whole log reaches it; and natively (`cargo run --bin replay`) both the session from its start and the save with the inputs after it reach it.
- **Asteroid belts and fields.** A system file's `belts` (schema-validated: a belt is a ring round the star or a world, a field a cluster on an orbit; width, density, up to three of the lab's ten compositions, a seed). Each is a core body of its own kind (`BODY_BELT`, `BODY_FIELD`): no well, no port, and nothing steers round it, so traffic and the autopilot fly through. The autopilot flies to a field's centre, or to the nearest point of a belt's ring, and stops there. The page places the rocks once from each belt's seed (scenery, not in the snapshot or hash): thinning toward a belt's edges and gathering in clumps along it, sizes on a power law from 15 to 500 m. They are drawn with the lab's asteroid shader, now shared (`src/rocks.js`; the lab's Asteroids tab uses it too) as one instanced draw for every rock in view; a rock under a pixel and a half across is a speck of light. Belts and fields take their place in the location list in orbital order (a field round a world among its children), with the range to a belt's nearer edge; the radar draws a belt as a band, the map as a ring, and the nav overlay, target brackets and panel follow its nearest point. Brannock has the Scree, a belt between Halde and Vast (about 9,000 rocks: silicate, carbonaceous, metallic), and Cinder Tailings, a field of iron and basalt round Cinder. `npm run check` checks that they clear every body and the jump radius; the play smoke test checks the list's order and flies the autopilot into the belt.
- **The Calder March and the debug jump.** Six systems in `src/systems/` (Brannock, and Ostry, Calder, Merrow, Serein and Quire from `src/lore/regions/calder-march.md`), each with lore and art, and each an entry in the universe index (`src/galaxy/universe.json`: all 929 systems of the map) that it must agree with. The page reads a system when the game first needs it (`src/data.js`). The core's atlas (`core/src/atlas.rs`) holds the layouts of the systems this game has been to, eight slots by star id (bodies, prices, traffic mix and params, saved with the world), and a system stored mid-session goes into the replay log as its slot's bytes, so a jump is a core command (`CMD_JUMP`): the old system's traffic and boards go, the new one's start, and the player arrives at rest over the chosen body, keeping credits, hold and missions (a mission is delivered only in the system it was taken for). Saves and replays follow jumps, natively too. The debug screen's jump box takes `place@system`, both sides completing (`kett@bra` finds Kettle Yard in Brannock); its Content tab shows the current system.
- **Twelve star classes and binaries.** The system schema's `star.class` is the lab's old Suns set: yellow, orange and red dwarfs, brown dwarf, red giant, white giant, blue supergiant, Wolf-Rayet, white dwarf, pulsar, black hole and quasar. `src/stars.js` holds each class's look, light (colour, ambient, the sky's tint) and game numbers (radius, well strength, how far the well reaches, and for the exotics a hazard zone); a system file can set radius, gravity and hazard per star and override any look value (`star.look`), and `star.companion` adds a second star on an orbit from the first (planets may orbit it). The game draws them with the shared star kit (the lab's shaders: granulation, spots, corona, prominences, pulsar beams, a ray-marched black hole that lenses the sky), and a binary lights each world by the nearer star most. A compact star's well reaches many of its radii (`bodies.reach`).
- **Hazard zones.** Pulsars (9 km), black holes (7.5 km) and quasars (11 km) have a zone where radiation wears a hull down by up to 8 to 15% of its sound points a second, most at the middle (core/src/service.rs). Traffic routes and the autopilot keep clear of it (the detour's clearance); the HUD shows the dose, the radar and map ring the zone, the location list tags the star. `npm run check` keeps zones off every port and the jump zones.
- **Fuel and hull.** Each class has a tank (1.5 t at class I to 60 t at class V, in the catalog) and hull points (the catalog's, times the line's). Fuel is for jumps. **Heat** (2026-10-17; core/src/service.rs; the Book's M2 Energy and heat, built small) is the burner's limit: a cold ship reaches full heat in `heat_burn` (15) seconds of burn, where the burner locks until heat is down to `heat_unlock` (half); not burning, it cools from full in `heat_cool` (10) seconds, faster in gas (`heat_gas`); radiation adds heat (`heat_rad`). Every hull holds the same heat for now. Hull and fuel show on the HUD and the port screen; heat on the HUD's assist line (amber over 70%, red and LOCKED at full).
- **Speeds.** Top speeds went up 1.5 times on 2026-10-16 and 1.25 times more on 2026-10-17 (class I 619 m/s down to class V 169; the Copperhaul about 373). The burner no longer raises the top speed: it gives 2.5 times the thrust up to the same top speed, so it gets you there, or stops you, faster.
- **Open space is fast** (2026-10-17; core/src/flight.rs). Outside every gravity well a hull's top speed rises with the distance to the nearest well's edge, up to `cruise_max` (3) times its top speed: the speed from which its thrust (times `cruise_brake`, 1) brakes back to top speed by that edge, so the long gaps are the fast ones and any hull slows in time; there the dampers bleed at `cruise_bleed` (1 a second), so speed follows the ceiling down as a well nears. ASSIST's set speed (its throttle times the top speed) rises and falls with it, the autopilot flies by it, and traffic paces by it on rails and under physics (its trip times planned with it, so the traffic counts hold). The HUD's top speed is the one where the ship is. With the jump radius fitted to each system, an average visit (in from a zone, one hop between ports, out by a zone) in the Copperhaul went from 4.7–6.5 minutes to 2.3–3.3.
- **Sensors** (2026-10-17; core/src/sensors.rs; the Book's M0 Signature and detection, and Radiation, ported from the lab into the core, so they save and replay). Every ship's signature each tick: hull length over 18 m to the 1.2, times idle, engine glow, burner and side thrusters, and for the player heat and an active ping; running cold (C) cuts it to about a third. SNR = `sens_k` x signature / range (km)^2 x gas transmission / local noise; the player's on every ship and every ship's on the player settle toward it (ships remember the player longest). At SNR 1 a ship is a contact (the radar and the view show an error circle), at 3 identified (a dot, its class and faction); the target (T and Shift+T cycle contacts only; a core command, as scans need it), identified and within 5 km, is scanned in 2.2 s: hull, what it is doing, whether it is tracking you. A target the sensors lose lapses. V pings (P is pause): sensor gain up to 6x for a few seconds, a wavefront on the view and radar, and a signature spike everyone hears; 6 s cooldown. **Stellar radiation**: each star's output (its brightness over a yellow dwarf's), dimmed in the shadow corridor behind every planet and moon, raises sensor noise and heats the ship (`heat_star`). **Flares**: the system's most flare-prone star (its look's flare activity) flares every 70 to 120 seconds over that, after 8 s of warning, for 9 s at up to 10x; the HUD counts down and says whether the ship is exposed or sheltered, and the radar shades the shadows. Right after launch from Halde about 11 of 43 ships are held, 26 after a ping. All the numbers are core params.
- **Refuel and repair.** Port commands (`CMD_REFUEL`, `CMD_REPAIR`; F and R in port): fill the tank at the port's fuel price (its market's, where it sells fuel, else the high tier), restore the hull at 2 cr a point, as far as the credits go; only where the port lists the service.
- **Death.** A hull at zero loses the ship (`MODE_DEAD`): the clock stops behind a dialog that reloads the last landing's autosave or quits to the title. A new game autosaves at its start port, so there is always a landing to go back to. A traffic ship lost to radiation is gone.
- **Moving stations.** Every station round a planet or moon, port and environmental, circles it on the game clock (core/src/motion.rs), paced as real orbits are: the system file's `stations.period` (900 s by default) is a turn for a station three of its world's radii out, and the period grows as distance to the power 1.5, so far stations turn slower and move slower. Saves, loads, jumps and replays find them where they should be. Orbits may pass through other bodies and each other (a moon, another station); nothing collides, so it is only drawn on top. Stations round the star hold still unless `stations.starPeriod` is set (their ring would sweep through every planet's neighbourhood): Kettle Yard and Quire Gate stay put. What is docked at a station moves with it: the player while landed or in a sequence, traffic launching and docking, and the ends of routes there (a loiter's whole loop); the ferries round a world turn with its stations. Landing speed is relative to it; a launch hands back control moving with it; near a moving port, full assist holds its set speed relative to it (the HUD shows the speed REL) and the autopilot flies relative to its target, so holding still over it is the assist's job and, with assist off, the pilot's. Port stations round worlds (Calder Station, Ostry Skim, Halo Clinics) move at 30 to 40 m/s, environmental stations nearer in a little faster.
- **Station looks.** Stations bake at twice their old resolution (0.3 to 0.8 m a texel; about 50 MB of textures a station bake, against 13 before), and round parts (rings, hubs, discs) are plated in courses round them, rather than in a grid laid across them, as are their patches, rivets and windows.
- **The Suns tab (debug screen).** The lab's Suns tab, moved into the game's debug screen: the system's stars and one of each class, drawn with the game's star kit and their lens flares; a click opens a star in the builder, whose sliders edit the system file's star entry and show it as JSON to copy.
- **Port lore on the port screen.** The card shows the lore's first paragraph; About (I) has the full description, notable places and the system's history and culture; the Bar (B) lists the people in tonight with their portraits, what they say, and the rumors overheard. The painted vista fades in over the procedural view of the body. The build inlines the lore, and the page loads the art from `art/` beside it: opened on its own, without that folder, the page falls back to the procedural view and the people's initials.

## Deviations from the Book

- **No array compaction yet.** Ship slots are stable with an alive mask; generation handles detect reuse. The Book's swap-remove compaction waits until no JS-owned columns run parallel to the core's.
- **Only stations move.** Planets, moons and the stars hold still, as in EV; a station with a `motion` orbits. Its spin is drawn only: it docks at its centre, not at a turning port on its rim. A binary's stars hold still too.
- **Fuel and damage come early.** The Book puts fuel in M4 and damage in M2; M1 has the tank, the burner's fuel and radiation damage so refuel, repair and death have something to do. Shields, which would soak radiation first, come with M2, so in M1 radiation goes straight to the hull.
- **The quasar is a star class.** The lab's twelve suns include a quasar, so the twelve classes do: a black hole feeding hard enough to throw jets, with the largest hazard zone.
- **Gravity is game physics,** by design: see `core/src/flight.rs`.
- **Prices are content, not core code.** The Book's markets are columns in the core; M1's fixed prices are computed in the page from `src/economy.json` and written into them, so M5's moving prices can replace the writer without changing the columns or the trading commands.
- **Missions are fixed code, not templates yet.** The Book's mission template (objective nodes, triggers, branches) is tagged M1, but M1 has one mission type, so delivery is written directly in `core/src/mission.rs`; the template machinery waits for a second type.
- **Only four factions have hulls.** The catalog has the Federation, Prism, the Ironhulls and the Bloom, each with a military and a civilian line (the Book's Ships chapter; names in `docs/roster.md`); the Blackhulls, Freeflag, the Glass and the Shadow wait for M3's rosters. The Federation's and the Bloom's civilian lines are their military grammar without weapons, until they are designed.
- **Traffic over the Book's budget.** The cap is 90 ships, not 40 to 60, and reaching it refuses spawns rather than loading each ship with more tonnage.
- **Docking is instant.** L at a station slides the ship in at once; the Book has it hold still while the clamps engage.
- **Masks branch.** The alive and rail masks are columns, but the kernels skip on them (`if alive == 0 { continue }`) rather than multiplying through them as the Book puts it.
- **Big hulls land.** Class IV and V container ships in the traffic mix land at ports; the Book (M3) has capital ships berth at large stations or send landers.
- **Traffic never meets the player.** Ships pass through each other and the player; no hails, scans or reactions until combat and law arrive.
- **Rocks are scenery.** Belt and field rocks are placed by the page from each belt's seed, outside the sim: ships fly through them, and nothing can hit, scan or mine one. Mining (M3) brings the rocks into the core. Their sizes stop at 500 m where the lab's go to 3 km: rocks that large would cover a belt. The lore was written before the belts, so it doesn't mention them (`tools/gen-content.js` leaves them out of its facts).
- **One price per commodity per port.** No buy and sell spread, as in EV: profit comes only from price gaps between ports.
